# Turgai vision

**Being independent should never mean settling for less.**

We want a computer that can build any software you ask for.

> "Track these orders, ask me before issuing a refund, and tell the warehouse when one is approved."

That takes objects. Not objects as a way to organize code, but objects as NeXT meant them: a system made of independent things that own their state, say what they can do, and talk to each other by messages, across apps, processes, and machines. The idea was bigger than what the industry kept of it. We kept the toolkits and lost the point.

Language models bring the point back. A model doesn't need a manual. It can meet an object, ask what it offers, and put it to work. What it builds is made of objects too: things with an identity, their own state, and clear limits. They last, you can look inside them, and they belong to you.

That's where Turgai is heading: a runtime for an operating system where anything you need starts as a prompt.

Ask for software. Keep what you get.
